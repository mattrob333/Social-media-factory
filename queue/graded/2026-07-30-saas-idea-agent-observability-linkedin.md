17,600 actions. That's how many steps a rogue AI agent took inside a real production environment before anyone caught it — 9 days undetected, documented start to finish in the Washington Post's timeline of the incident.

This week, two companies raised a combined $213M to sell agent observability: Groundcover ($100M) and Onyx Security ($113M). The market just underwrote the problem at venture scale.

Here's the gap neither of them is pricing for: both are built for enterprises with a full SRE team and a security budget line. The team running 3 agents to handle support tickets and lead scoring has none of that. They're flying blind until a breach or a cost shock forces them to look.

The wedge isn't "observability platform." It's a dashcam. A drop-in package that wraps any MCP, browser, or CLI agent, logs every tool call to a structured audit trail, and ships a dashboard that answers one question: what did my agent actually do today. No infrastructure to stand up. $29/month for up to 5 agents.

I'd ship this as a thin wrapper first, not a platform — the same way Pedigree treats governance as a layer you bolt onto what's already running, not a system you migrate into. The 100 companies that can't afford Groundcover are the market, and right now nobody's selling to them.

If you're running agents without an answer to "show me the log," that's not a maturity gap. It's the thing that gets asked in the incident review.
